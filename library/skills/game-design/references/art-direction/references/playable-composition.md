# Compose a playable view with line, light and dark

A ferry has reached the quay. The player must pull a ring to lower the gangway, then walk aboard. Which part of the picture should they notice first? The answer changes as soon as the gangway lands. Make a pair of sketches for those two moments before spending time on rope textures or water reflections.

## Find the route in a few lines

Draw a box in the same proportions as the game window. Inside it, draw only the deck edge, the gangway, the post with the ring, and the player's outline. Look at the shape of the air between the ring and the ship. If a railing crosses its center, the ring can read as part of the fence. Move the post sideways until you can see through the ring. Check from both ends of the quay.

Make two more frames using the same objects. In one, repeat the railing posts at equal intervals. In the other, leave a larger gap at the handle. Even spacing gives the pier a steady rhythm. The break can single out the control, but the gap might also look like an invitation to step into the water. Keep the low safety rail continuous in both sketches.

## Try two values, then three

A *value* is how light or dark an area looks. Trace the same view twice and fill each enclosed area black or white. Give the water the light area in one version and the deck the light area in the other. Set the ring against an area of the opposite value. Set the sketches a few steps away. Where do your eyes land? Which view lets you trace a continuous walk from the player to the ring? Switching which shapes carry the black can change both answers without moving an object.

Add one middle gray. Try it on the water to reduce the pull of bright reflections. Save the strongest light-dark edge for the handle before the action. After the gangway lands, show the new connection by bringing its walking surface close in value to the deck. Let a dark edge along the sides carry its outline. If the ring still commands attention, turn it flat against the post when the mechanism latches.

## Test the change in play

Tint the approved grays with a few colors. Check a grayscale capture after tinting. A red ring and green post can merge if they are equally light. If the ring vanishes, lighten its backing or restore the gap inside its silhouette. Test at the intended display size with the usual interface visible.

Give a fresh player the goal “board the ferry.” Watch what they approach first and where they step when the gangway lands. If they go straight to the gap and miss the ring, try the equal-spaced post version with the handle still visible. If they approach the ring and walk away, expose the rope that connects it to the gangway so they can infer what pulling it will do. If they pull the ring and wait, compare the two gangway positions from the player's camera. Keep the edit that fixes the observed mistake on the next fresh attempt.

## Take the same test into a different frame

- **Approach, use and departure.** Lay out three views of the same ferry, drawn from the places players actually occupy. Overlay the usual interface and try the narrowest supported view. If the handle needs a wider clearing in only one frame, move the background bracket that crosses it there. Save a stronger change of scale or placement for a cue that disappears on most approaches.
- **Large shapes before fine edges.** Reduce a figure to its shoulder, wrist and the thing it carries. Try the active pose as one dark shape and again with a gap at the elbow. Check the smallest view before deciding whether the line of the sleeve needs a second drawing. For a snowy yard where the worker and the fence merge, add a middle value behind the figure. Check which separation that value bought before adding another.
- **Repeating edges.** Sketch three signs in the same rectangular frame. Give each trade a different silhouette or direction of opening. Place the signs in a busy row and reduce them to play size. Check a repeating floor or fence where four tiles meet. A shape that accidentally joins into an arrow can send a player toward the wrong turn. Redraw just the join and retest the approach.
