# Design map symbols and labels at each scale

Write the question the player will open the map to answer. A moving player steering a cart mainly needs the next entrance. At a stop, the same player may need to choose between two routes on different floors. Start with two views that answer those questions well and then see which marks can carry through both.

## Deliver a lens to the observatory

The player pushes a telescope lens from Clock Court to the Dome. The Stone Ramp and Goods Lift both reach Upper Gallery. A staff stair also connects the floors. The cart fits on the ramp and in the lift. The ramp is open. The map records a keeper's report that the lift was running yesterday. Only a visit confirms whether it runs today.

| View and use | Draw first | Leave for the closer view |
| --- | --- | --- |
| District atlas for planning | Clock Court, Upper Gallery and the Dome. Connect them with the two named cart routes. Attach the dated report to the lift entry. | Individual rooms, door swings and railing detail. |
| Floor plan for finding an entrance | Two named floors, the three separate entrances, and each upper exit. Put a carriage symbol at both lift doors and a stepped line at the stair. Keep the door name beside the correct threshold as the view rotates. | Individual counters and benches, unless one serves as an entrance landmark. |
| Minimap for steering | The cart's facing, the next usable turn and the step mark where players might confuse the stair with the route. | Distant rooms and yesterday's report. Keep the report available on the selected lift in the planning view. |

Put an equivalent text route beside the view. Reuse the same place names. Give instructions from the marked point of approach, such as “From Clock Court, take Stone Ramp to Upper Gallery.” Update the displayed state wherever the old report appears if the player checks the lift. Keep the difference between uncertain service and an uncertain location clear. The shaft is in a known place.

Use one symbol for each decision until the set can be read without help. Try a line style for a stair, an icon for a lift and a direct label for an unusual door. Check a label near two adjacent doors by covering both symbols and asking which entrance the text belongs to. Reposition a floating label if its anchor is ambiguous. Check the layout again at the smallest supported text size and with the map facing the other way.

## Compare at the court

| Ask the player to | Record and use the answer |
| --- | --- |
| Point to Goods Lift from the paused map. | If they read the name and select the stair, revise the label's attachment to the threshold. |
| Choose today's cart route and describe what they know about the other route. | A player who checks the lift despite the dated report has made a choice. If they believe the report guarantees service, change the status text. |
| Take the turn while moving. | Record the first frame where the cue can be read and the last point where steering can still work. If the floor plan works and the moving turn fails, compare the cue's timing at the actual speed. |
| Travel back, then make the trip on foot. | Verify that the same symbols still describe the world and that the staff stair is offered on foot. |

Check grayscale, high contrast and any available text or spoken route on the intended device. Use a real traversal to check cart clearance, an actionable door and a reachable exit. Those checks establish facts a well-drawn map can only communicate.

For a map that also shows snags, decide whether a marker means number of snags or chance per delivery. In a sketch, East Yard has 8 jams in 40 trips and West Yard has 10 in 200. The chance is 20% in East Yard and 5% in West Yard. Mark a yard with zero trips as unobserved. Ask a player which route they would take for a fragile load and what the mark tells them.
