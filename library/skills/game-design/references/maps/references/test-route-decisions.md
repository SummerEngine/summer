# Test a route decision

Draw the fork you want to test. Mark the earliest place the two routes are visible, the last place a player can switch without retracing their steps, and where the routes meet again. Write one action the player should be able to take there, such as choosing a direct delivery or a deliberate detour. A count of left and right turns needs that purpose before it can guide an edit.

## Build the Cloud Dyehouse

The player brings a spool to the blue loom. An upper catwalk goes straight there. A lower walkway passes dye vats where the player can take a color sample, then joins the catwalk at a lift. Use the actual walking speeds to estimate how much time the lower route adds. Put the samples far enough down the lower walkway that taking one is a decision.

In layout A, a broad drying sheet hides the catwalk's approach to the loom from the entrance. In B, raise the sheet just enough to show the final span from the fork. Keep the start position, light, route lengths and rewards fixed. Capture both views from the game's camera at the same spot. First, walk the routes and ride the lift. Then try the upper approach with the narrowest supported field of view.

## Observe the choice

Give each first-time visitor the same delivery request and one layout. Avoid naming either route in the prompt. Mark the first entry to the fork, the chosen branch, any reversal and the arrival at the loom. Record where the sample was collected if the player visits the vats. Use a camera recording to see whether the connection was in view. After the player commits, ask where they expected the path to lead and what drew them there.

| Result | Next edit or check |
| --- | --- |
| Visitors in B describe the continuous high route more often and fewer turn back after reaching the vats by surprise. | Keep the opening in the sheet. Check that dressing and lighting preserve it from the doorway. |
| A visitor knows the lower path is slower and takes it to get a sample. | Keep the choice. Compare whether first visitors recognize the tradeoff. |
| A visitor identifies the loom and assumes the catwalk is scenery. | Keep the landmark in view. In the next comparison, extend the walkable floor finish through the catwalk entrance. |
| A visitor stops at the fork and later says they were watching the dye spill. | Check if they resume in the intended direction when the spill ends. A pause has several possible causes. |
| A visitor chooses correctly but misses the lift control. | Test the sign or interaction at the lift. The first branch served its purpose. |

## Read the record

Summarize each version as counts out of first unassisted visits. Include correct route descriptions, deliberate detours and reversals caused by a wrong expectation. Track retries separately. Someone already sent back to the entrance has learned something about the route. If a heatmap shows a knot at the fork, inspect whole visits around that mark. One player circling can make several marks. Position alone leaves gaze and intent open.

Keep cases with missing footage or an uncertain explanation visible in the summary. With a small group, a single account can suggest the next comparison. Ask fresh players to try that revision before treating the change as a dependable improvement.
