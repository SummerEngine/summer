# Spells and visual effects

Start with the moment someone will remember. “I held the last note after my friend took their hands off the organ” gives you a caster, a partner, an action, and something a picture can show. An environmental effect may have no caster or decision at all. A puff of flour settling on a sleeping baker can earn its place with a good sight gag. Keep creator replies free of book citations and reading lists.

## Find the idea

For a direct pitch, make a proposal the creator can use immediately. Take one evocative detail and give it a consequence. A magician who hems shadows could pin a shadow to the last sunlit tile, or tuck a small object into someone else's shadow for them to carry. The first asks where the light will move. The second asks whose route will take the object where it belongs. Give the creator a reason to choose, and follow the idea they like.

In an interview, ask one question per turn. “When the trick works, what do you want the other player to do?” can establish whether an empty reflection should invite a partner to step in or tempt a rival to waste a turn. Follow their answer with the one remaining uncertainty that would most change the design. A pitch that already settles this deserves a sketch or rule. For a family of spells, keep one principle the player can learn, such as every borrowed thing returning to its owner, and change the situation in which that principle helps. Ask what happens when two spells meet.

## Write the promise the picture makes

For an ability, play one use on paper. Describe who can act, what they aim at, what they give up, the instant the game commits, what another player can do in time, and what ends the effect. Include a miss, cancellation, repeated use or contested target when it changes the decision. For an environmental effect, identify its physical source, what sets it moving, what stops or repeats, and whether anyone should infer a game rule from it. Let atmosphere remain atmosphere when that is its job.

Draw the few poses that express those changes at the normal play size. During aim, show the caster whatever they can still change. At commitment, let the affected player see the source or footprint in time to respond. At contact, put the clearest change **where the rule takes effect**. While a state persists, give it a form someone can recognize arriving halfway through. When it ends, let the actionable shape disappear on the same update as the rule. A curl of paper can drift away from a closed tile if it looks like spent paper.

Choose a visual gesture that belongs to this fiction. Wax sags under its own weight. A ribbon pulled by one end goes straight there first. Break the gesture into unequal beats. Hold enough of the outline still that players can track it while the moving part tells them what is changing. Spend the sharpest edge, contrast or fastest motion on the current focal point. Try the cast behind a rival's warning, across a bright floor, and beneath the HUD. Reduce a competing halo or move a flourish toward its source before making both effects brighter.

## Review what was supplied

Name the frame, time, viewpoint or game event you actually examined. Describe what is visible and the action it seems to invite. State a competing explanation, change one thing, and say which result would change your advice. A still can reveal that a shadow covers the far edge. A timed sequence can measure how long the edge is visible. To learn whether a new player uses that time, watch their first attempt and ask what they thought they could do. Preserve something the creator already does well.

Build the smallest version that can answer the open question. Cut out the shapes or use flat sprites and one shared animation clock. Try muted audio, a still or reduced-motion cue, and a background that shares the cue's hue. Keep the essential distinction legible by outline, mark, position or text as appropriate. Measure representative hardware with repeated casts and the busiest plausible overlap. Compare frame time and the cost of large transparent layers with detail enabled and disabled. Record recognition or mistaken actions separately from rendering measurements. For requested Godot particle or material implementation, use the matching VFX skill from Summer's library.
