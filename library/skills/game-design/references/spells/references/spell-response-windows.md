# Give a spell a response window

Mark when the attempt becomes public, what has been spent at that moment, who may respond, and what the game checks when the effect happens. A paper rehearsal can reveal whether another player actually has a decision in the interval.

## Rehearse an encore at the puppet theatre

The singer wants an onstage puppet to bow. The manager wants the puppet in the wings before the closing bell. Start with one puppet, one singer's ticket and one manager's cue card. Use two beats before the bell. At each beat, the singer can offer the ticket for an encore or wait. If the singer offers it, put it face up beside the puppet's portrait and consume it. Then the manager can spend their card to call Curtain or wait. The manager can also call Curtain on a beat when the singer waits.

Curtain moves the named puppet to the wings before the end-of-beat bow check. Encore earns a star at that check if the named puppet is still onstage. The manager earns a star if the puppet reaches the wings by the bell. Use the same two beats with each outcome. After an Encore attempt, the singer has spent the ticket and can only wait. This rehearsal gives the manager one distinct choice per beat and resolves both roles' effects at a known point.

| What the players do | What happens | Decision to discuss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| On beat one the singer offers the ticket and the manager calls Curtain. | The puppet exits and the encore earns no star. | Announcing the encore gave the manager a precise time to intervene. |
| The singer offers the ticket and the manager waits until beat two. | The puppet bows and earns the singer's star. Curtain on beat two earns the manager's star as well. | The manager can allow the bow and still finish the scene. |
| The singer waits and the manager calls Curtain on beat one. | The puppet exits. There is no eligible puppet on beat two. The ticket is unspent. | Waiting can preserve a resource while losing the target. |
| Both wait on beat two. | The bell ends the scene. No pending action remains. | Passing on the last beat closes the chance to act. |

For the first rehearsal, the manager may prefer to spoil the bow every time. Give them a scene variation where pulling the puppet on beat one cuts off a musician they want to hear. Retain the single reaction and check whether they now weigh the bow against the music. If the scene is about cooperation, shared points for finishing both jobs can support the second order.

## Make each beat legible

Give the singer a private aim preview. On announcement, place a bow token by the portrait and show the spent ticket to both players. Open the response window with a clear prompt naming the puppet. When the manager spends the card, start the puppet's trip to the wings. Add a singer's star on a bow and a manager's star at the exit. If the named puppet has left, close the bow token on its portrait at the check. A second puppet onstage gives no new target unless the design explicitly allows retargeting.

Pause the rehearsal at announcement. Ask each player which resource is gone, what response is possible and whether a star has been awarded. Use blank scores and props at first. Test with two eligible puppets, a puppet that leaves before an announcement, and a timed window that expires without a response. If test players mistake the ticket tear for success, move the applause to the bow and try the same order again. Compare whether they can identify the remaining decision at the intended play speed.
