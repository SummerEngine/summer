# Keep overlapping effects readable

In a pottery game, the kiln bell rings over a disk of floor and strips the glaze from every clay figure inside. Meanwhile, one partner can repair a chipped pot. Draw a figure one step inside the disk and another safely outside. Show both the warning and the repair from the play camera. Ask what each figure can do next, then step through the result.

## Draw the floor first

Trace the disk used by the game onto the tiles. Give the rim a clear shape from either side of the bell. A faint fill can tell the player that the whole area inside is affected. Add the swinging bell after the rim reads. Keep the rim's radius fixed if the game already knows which tiles will be affected. For countdown motion, join breaks in the rim as the bell swings. Use a few visible steps if smooth motion disappears at play size.

Give the repair a scalloped mark around the pot and one short gleam where the crack closes. Give the kiln rim inward-pointing chips. Use color to support both cues. On light clay tiles, try a narrow dark edge under the rim. On soot, check the silhouette and find an outline that still reads. Preserve critical edges when a character or a foreground prop overlaps the warning. Test the far side of the bell and the edge of the viewport.

| Beat | Kiln warning | Repair effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before the strike | Show the full rim and the state of the countdown. A step across the rim can still matter. | Keep the mark small and attached to the pot. |
| Strike | Flake the glaze from affected figures and close the warning at the occupancy check. | Give the repaired pot a brief confirmation. |
| After the strike | Let a few matte chips settle. They can cross the old rim once it has closed. | Remove the repair mark once the new pot state is visible. |
| Different rule, lingering heat | Keep a recognizable floor boundary through the last harmful update. Dim the burst itself once it has done its job. | Keep the same attachment and timing. |

Give thrown objects a different job. For a moving clay seal, try a distinct leading edge and a taper in the wake. Show it without a caster in frame and ask someone which way it is moving. Save small glaze cracks or maker's marks for close views.

## Test the warning with company

Place the repair over the kiln rim in a busy frame. Run the same case on pale and dark tiles, in grayscale, at the smallest expected screen size, and with reduced motion. Ask a first-time viewer which area they would enter, where they would step to get safe, and when they would go back for the pot. Record which floor position they choose and which update they call safe. Compare to the underlying state. In play, note whether someone recognizes the exit and has time to reach it before the bell lands.

If viewers can trace the boundary but mistake the repair for danger, strengthen the difference in shape and where each cue attaches. If they cannot trace the boundary, isolate the overlap and change the edge treatment there. If they return during lingering heat, restore a visible cue for the active state. Save one representative view before and after the edit so the artist can see what the revision preserved.
