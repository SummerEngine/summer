# Give a spell a cost worth considering

Write the changed fact in one line. Follow it with the resource spent, when it is spent, and how the player can recover it. Sketch one situation where casting is attractive and one where saving it is attractive. Show the player the information that makes these choices possible.

## Send a courier across the canal

The ferry rings a watch bell on departure. A guard who hears it checks the quay. The courier can press a wax seal onto the reachable bell and delay its next ring by eight seconds. The seal is consumed when it is attached. A longer towpath takes the passenger across without using the seal. From a clear vantage, a guard can still see the passenger. Test the ferry both with a warehouse screening the boat and with a guard on the open bridge.

| State | Rule in the prototype | Cue at play size |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Choosing | Inspecting the bell or walking away keeps the seal. A bell with a seal already attached cannot take another. | Show the local action “Seal next ring” and preview “Rings here 8 seconds later.” |
| Armed | A valid attachment spends one seal. The clock starts at the next departure. | The wax is smooth and the inventory count drops. |
| Holding a sound | The clapper's first strike starts the eight-second delay. Further bell triggers during the delay produce an ordinary ring. | A crease grows in the wax. Explain a second trigger if the level allows one. |
| Released | The trapped ring sounds at the bell. Any guard who hears it applies the usual investigation rule. | Split the wax, swing the clapper and show the same directional sound cue used for an ordinary ring. |

Give a first-time player the seal and let them inspect the patrol from the bank. Ask where the guard will go if the bell rings and where the passenger might be seen. Let them try and ask what made them choose. If the boat looks invisible while the seal is active, keep the effect on the bell and replay the bridge approach. If everyone saves the seal because the towpath is always quicker, time both crossings. Change a decision cue or the route cost and try one new build. With several such encounters, include a later use the player can reasonably anticipate.

Test an invalid interaction, a cancellation before attachment, the first departure and a potential second departure. Test expiry after the player leaves earshot. Make sure the guard's investigation and the clapper read from the same event. If someone can skip the trip by repeatedly interacting with the attached seal, decide whether it is an interesting trick or a broken commitment rule before sealing that interaction.

## Price an experiment differently

A restorer rotates three panes until the leadwork forms a heron. Early quarter-turns help them discover which lines belong to each pane. Start with unlimited turns. Keep the selected frame attached to the rotating pane so a player can follow one change at a time. Ask them to predict which pieces the next turn will bring together. If they understand the motion and still hesitate, check the cue that says they can undo or try again.

A refill, a cooldown and a consumed item give players different reasons to wait. If you add a limit to a spell, make a specific next decision possible with it. Test whether the player can state what they gave up and why the attempt was worth it.
