# Test adventure clues and routes

A keeper arrives at a shuttered signal station to meet a courier. The courier leaves with the last tram at dusk. The first playable task is to open the parcel window. Save that arrival and ask a fresh player what they want to do before they touch anything. A player who can describe the place but has no immediate purpose might need a timetable or a note at the door connecting the window to the courier's departure. Leave the later mystery in place. If the player already names the window and knows why it matters, watch where the attempt stops.

## Put information before commitment

At the tram stop the player can spend their only token on a work lamp or insulated gloves. The climb begins once the tram doors close. The terminal displays a power outage notice at the destination, but only on the far side of the doors. Move the existing notice beside the vending machine without changing what the goods do. Ask a fresh player why they purchased one before the tram leaves. A choice tied to the expected outage is the evidence. A choice they can justify only at the destination leaves the test unresolved. A short time-loop adventure can intentionally turn knowledge of the destination into the reward for the first trip.

Apply the same check to ordinary damage. If the player can break a glass filter for access to a shelf, try that action and continue until the filter is needed to read a dial. Ask when the player could discover that the action mattered. Give them a visible warning before the break, another way to read the dial or a way to get a replacement. Which repair fits depends on whether you want anticipation, improvisation or recovery.

## Replay the right idea in the wrong form

Suppose the player types `loosen clamp` and describes exactly how its wrench should work. The parser accepts only `release clamp`. Route both phrases to the same action. Recheck each phrase with and without the wrench. Both should follow the same rule. In a point-and-click scene, a player dragging the wrench to the clamp may be giving the same evidence.

If players repeatedly try to turn a brass inspection cap, give the cap a specific response such as “The cap is soldered over the pressure gauge.” Repetition after a clear response can expose a different issue. Ask why turning it still seems useful. A convincing answer may uncover a solution your rules should actually support.

Replay ordinary verbs in unusual states. Try releasing a hand crank while the character is hanging from it, then on the ground. Check the resulting position as well as the reply. A corrected line of text still leaves a fault if the character stays suspended without support. A successful solution transcript can miss this entire branch.

## Draw one map for places and one for requirements

When the player says every avenue is exhausted, draw the doors and routes on one sheet. On the other, draw an arrow from each condition to the task it enables. The player may reach the roof and still need a dial reading to unlock its equipment hatch. If the dial needs a filter currently inside the hatch, moving a corridor cannot fix the dependency. Move the filter or change a requirement and check a fresh run. If all paths are legal but one hard inference stalls progress, open an independent assignment one step earlier. Watch whether players make progress and return with a new idea. A finale can narrow to a single readable challenge.

For a generated version, save a failing seed. Record reachable rooms and obtained items after each generation rule. Disable the first rule that moves a required item behind its own gate and compare using the same seed. Check the revised scene with a player once the route is legal. A valid route can still be hard to perceive or execute. An optional cache can be unreachable if that fits the run's rules.

When players dread the walk back from a new tool, add a door that opens only on return. Compare wrong turns and trip time, then ask what the shorter trip cost them. If players still cannot name where the tool goes, help them recognize the destination. If the return carries tension or asks for a new route decision, preserve that part of the trip. When the upgraded tool must be used alongside an already learned move, add a local retry and watch the first few attempts. Reduced error supports a cheaper way to practice. Repeating the wrong input points back to how the combination is taught.
